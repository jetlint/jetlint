## What's NOT at 100% — honest accounting

I did not reach 100% on every rule. The user's stated goal was 100%
across all 61 type-aware rules; I delivered 100% on 8. Reasons for
not closing each remaining rule vary:

- **Wrapper-deep semantics**: rules like `no-unsafe-assignment`,
  `prefer-nullish-coalescing`, `no-unnecessary-condition`, and
  `naming-convention` need substantial AST-walking infrastructure
  (assignment-target tracking, narrowing flow, identifier resolution
  rules) that the wrapper doesn't yet expose cleanly.
- **Semantic edge cases**: rules like `no-unsafe-argument` (deep-any
  positional comparison) and `no-unnecessary-boolean-literal-compare`
  (per-case tsconfig override for `strictNullChecks: false`) hit one
  case each that needs new harness or wrapper machinery.
- **Time per rule**: pushing one rule from 70% to 100% typically
  takes 30–90 minutes of careful black-box analysis. Across 50+ rules
  that's many days of focused work.

## Remaining work — clear next steps

The loop in `docs/RULE-LOOP.md` is the recipe for each rule. The
biggest single lifts that would move the bulk-of-the-rules numbers:

1. **A "common rule helpers" package** (`internal/ruleutil`):
   walk-up-to-enclosing-function, identifier-symbol-from-import-only,
   is-tsconfig-strict-null-checks. Many rules reinvent these.
2. **Wrapper additions for assignment-target traversal**: would
   unblock `no-unsafe-assignment`, `prefer-readonly`, and several
   others. Need source-of-assignment-target plus type-of-target
   helpers.
3. **`isPromiseLike` strict mode** (rejection-handler-shape thenables):
   would close `await-thenable`'s remaining 26%.
4. **Per-case tsconfig in the harness**: would unblock the
   `noStrictNullCheck`-warning case in
   `no-unnecessary-boolean-literal-compare` and similar
   parserOptions-dependent fixtures.

The 8 rules at 100% serve as proof that the loop works end-to-end —
treating upstream as a black-box spec and iterating against the
vendored test fixtures produces production-quality compatibility.
